The Retailer's Reality

Is Your Gold Working for You…
or Against You?

In today's retail jewellery scenario, the stock standing in your showcase is either earning for you or quietly costing you. These are the pressures every jewellery retailer is carrying right now.

The 8 Core Operational Challenges Facing Today's Jewellery Retailers
01

Capital Blockage & Dead Inventory

Large amounts of capital remain locked in unsold gold inventory, restricting liquidity and slowing business growth.

02

Gold Price Volatility

Fluctuating gold prices can reduce profit margins when inventory is purchased at higher prices and sold after market corrections.

03

Burglary, Theft & Security Risk

High-value gold inventory increases exposure to burglary, theft, employee pilferage and operational risk.

04

High Inventory Holding Costs

Insurance, vault facilities, security systems, storage and inventory maintenance significantly increase operating expenses.

05

High Making Charges

Smaller manufacturing volumes often result in higher making charges, reducing profitability and price competitiveness.

06

Limited Product Range & Price Options

Stocking designs at multiple price points ties up capital, limiting variety across customer tastes and budgets.

07

Rapidly Changing Market Trends

Fast-changing consumer preferences make existing inventory outdated, increasing the risk of slow-moving or unsold stock.

08

Limited Customisation Flexibility

Ready-made stock restricts changes to size, purity, weight and finish, limiting personalisation to customer preferences.

There Is a Better Way to Sell Jewellery.

KARATS SMART MODEL

Sell First.
Buy Later.

The Traditional Model — Buy First

In the traditional model, retailers purchase finished gold ornaments, pay making charges and hold them as inventory before customer demand is confirmed. Every showcased design locks capital into a specific style, purity, weight and size while waiting for a customer whose taste and budget match it. Until it sells, the retailer bears finance, insurance, security and storage costs, along with the risks of changing trends and gold prices. The displayed range is limited by available capital, while replacing sold or outdated pieces requires further investment and keeps working capital tied to inventory.

The Traditional Retail Cycle
1. Purchasing Gold Ornaments
Gold ornaments are purchased according to estimated future demand before any customer or confirmed order exists. Cash leaves the business immediately, locking working capital in finished stock and exposing the retailer to changes in the gold price.
2. Display Gold Jewellery
Every displayed ornament occupies showcase space and continues to attract insurance, security and storage costs, . While it remains unsold, the retailer carries both the holding cost and gold-price exposure that may erode the expected margin.
3. Wait for Right Customer
Each ornament must wait for a customer whose taste, size, budget, design, weight and purity match it. During this period, preferences may change, the design may age and the piece may remain idle for months, delaying recovery of the invested capital.
4. Sale Completed
Capital returns only when the ornament is finally sold. Any gain from a higher gold price is only an illusionary profit. The same gold must be repurchased at the new higher rate for restocking. After holding costs and negotiations, the actual margin may be much smaller.
5. Discount / Remelt Unsold Stock
Pieces that still do not sell must be discounted, exchanged or returned for melting. The retailer absorbs lost making charges, wastage, handling and time. Fresh capital must then be committed to replace outdated stock, restarting the same inventory cycle.
The Karats Smart Model — Sell First

In the KARATS SMART model, you sell before investing in gold inventory. The counter displays precision-crafted 925 gold-plated silver ornaments. They reproduce the form, detail and finish of the final gold ornament. At roughly 30 times cheaper the cost of gold ornaments, they require far less display investment. Customers can see, touch and try each design before placing an order. They select their preferred purity, weight, stones, size, colour and finish. Production begins only after the order and advance are confirmed. Every gold purchase is therefore linked to a real sale, avoiding unsold gold inventory.

The Karats Smart Cycle
1. Purchase & Display of Gold-Plated Silver Ornaments
The showcase carries precisely finished, gold-plated 925 silver prototypes at a fraction of the cost of gold stock. Lower display investment allows the retailer to present a broader collection and refresh designs more frequently.
2. Customer Selects & Customises
The customer handles and tries the physical sample, then personalises purity, weight, stones, size, colour and finish. The same design can be quoted across five price ranges, making it easier to match individual tastes and budgets.
3. Order Confirmed
A final specification and transparent price are approved at the prevailing rate. The customer pays an advance and the order is booked, giving the retailer confirmed demand before committing capital to gold.
4. Manufacture the Ornament
Only the required quantity of gold is procured for the confirmed order. Skilled production then recreates the selected prototype to the customer's exact specifications, keeping material, labour and working capital tied to a sale.
5. Delivered & Sale Completed
The finished ornament is hallmarked, quality checked and delivered against the confirmed order. The balance is collected and the transaction closes without leaving an unsold gold piece, ageing design or blocked investment behind.

The Answer

8 Challenges.
8 SMART Answers.

How the KARATS SMART model overcomes each challenge — and the benefit it creates

01Reduced Capital Blockage & Dead Inventory

Challenge: Capital remains locked in unsold gold.
How: Gold is purchased only after a customer confirms the order.
Benefit: Working capital stays liquid and no unsold gold becomes dead inventory.

02Reduced Gold Price Volatility Risk

Challenge: Price changes can erode the margin on held inventory.
How: Gold is quoted and procured against the same confirmed order at the prevailing rate.
Benefit: The exposure window narrows and margins become more predictable.

03Reduced Burglary & Theft Risk

Challenge: High-value gold stock increases theft and security exposure.
How: The counter displays silver prototypes while gold is held only for confirmed orders.
Benefit: Far less bullion value remains exposed at the retail counter.

04Lower Inventory Holding Costs

Challenge: Gold inventory creates high insurance, security and storage costs.
How: High-value finished stock is replaced with low-cost silver prototypes.
Benefit: Insurance, vault, security, storage and maintenance expenses fall.

05Lower Making Charges & Stronger Customer Goodwill

Challenge: Small production runs increase making charges and reduce competitiveness.
How: Confirmed-order manufacturing reduces speculative production, remakes and wastage.
Benefit: Lower costs support stronger margins and fairer customer pricing.

06Wider Collections & Five Price Options

Challenge: Gold stock limits design variety and affordable price choices.
How: Low-cost prototypes offer each design in 22KT, 18KT, 9KT, silver and plated silver.
Benefit: A wider collection serves more budgets without blocking capital.

07Faster Rotation & Trend Adaptability

Challenge: Fast-changing trends can leave gold designs outdated and unsold.
How: Silver prototypes are refreshed quickly while gold is produced only after sale.
Benefit: The collection follows current demand without obsolete gold inventory.

08360° Jewellery Customisation

Challenge: Ready-made stock restricts personalisation to customer preferences.
How: Purity, weight, size, stones, colour and finish are finalised before production.
Benefit: Customers receive a personalised piece, improving satisfaction and conversion.
